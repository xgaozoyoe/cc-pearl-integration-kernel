Hypothesis: expand benefit of early stage publication by changing warp cohorts,
without changing compute ownership, operand reuse, hot-loop instructions, registers,
shared storage, arrival point, or occupancy.

Controls: validated tail9 and latest id16, both6+6 warps.
Candidates: id16 with4+8,8+4,3+9,9+3 contiguous warp cohorts.
All group boundaries are warp-aligned. No divergent named barrier within a warp.
Only prologue ISETP threshold changes. Disassembled main loop is byte-identical to id16.
Barrier count remains384 for both groups: N warps arrive on peer barrier and
12-N warps sync on that same barrier; the opposite barrier has reversed roles.
Each barrier therefore still covers all384 threads. IDs alternate4/5 then6/7.
Publication still waits for copy completion and all shared reads. A group may
proceed only after all other threads have published. Next publication cannot
complete until the slower group reaches its corresponding next sync.
This preserves stage reuse safety for unequal groups as for6+6.

Not a dedicated-producer design: all12 warps still do their identical IMMA work.
No added main-loop overhead. Candidate differences are scheduling/handoff grouping.
Offline only; production and known-good cubins are unchanged.
